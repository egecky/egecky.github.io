Research

My research develops theory and algorithms for reinforcement learning and stochastic decision-making. I study distributional and average-reward RL, multi-objective and risk-sensitive decision-making, and coupled-dynamics environments where actions have correlated outcomes.

A second line studies stochastic approximation and optimization under dependent data and weak noise assumptions. Recent work includes categorical distributional temporal-difference learning, non-expansive fixed-point problems with Markovian samples, and non-convex optimization under unbounded variance.

I also work on the theory and practice of LLM post-training. Our recent work analyzes the mean update and finite-rank variance of low-rank evolution strategies, and introduces LOO-ROLL, which uses a leave-one-out baseline to make more efficient use of model evaluations.

Earlier in my PhD, I worked on robust and submodular optimization, with applications to sensor selection, multi-task subset selection, federated learning, and distributed online optimization.
